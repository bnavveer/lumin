Almost every program uses the same yardstick: the federal poverty line. Put in your household size and yearly income, and the ladder below shows which programs your income fits under. It also shows which ones need something extra, like kids, a pregnancy, age or a disability.

California changes in 2026–2027

State budget cuts and the 2025 federal law hit California programs this year. Some changes haven't started yet.

Jan 2026

Medi-Cal asset limit is back for seniors and people with disabilities: $130,000 for 1 person, +$65,000 for each extra person. Most working-age adults still have no asset test.

Jan 2026

Undocumented adults 19+ can't newly enroll in full Medi-Cal. People already on it keep it if they renew on time. Children and pregnant people are not affected.

Apr–Jun 2026

CalFresh tightened. Refugees, asylees and some other immigrants lost federal eligibility (about 72,000 people, per the LAO). The 80-hour work rule for adults 18–64 without a child under 14 began in most counties.

Jul 2026

Routine dental ends for undocumented adults on Medi-Cal (one state page says 2027). The CalFresh fruit-and-vegetable bonus is paused because its funding ran out.

Jan 1, 2027

Medi-Cal work rule and renewals every 6 months start for expansion adults aged 19–64.

Jul 1, 2027

$30/month Medi-Cal premium starts for undocumented adults aged 19–59.

Oct 2027

CFAP food aid for undocumented adults 55+ is now scheduled for this date, after delays. It still needs budget funding.

One "yes" opens other doors

Once you're approved for one of four "gateway" programs, other programs often skip their own income check and accept that approval as proof. So apply for SNAP or Medicaid first.

Doors that depend on your situation

Some programs aren't tied to the poverty line, or only open for certain people. Check every card that fits you.

Anyone with low income base

  • SNAP (food stamps)Up to $306/mo for 1 person, $1,023/mo for 4 (from Oct 2026). Assets about $3,000, but most states waive the asset test.
  • Section 8 Housing Choice VoucherIncome at or below 50% of your county's median income. You pay about 30% of your income and the voucher pays the rest. Waitlists are often years long, and many are closed.
  • Public housingUp to 80% of area median income, with priority for the lowest incomes. Limits vary by county. Look yours up on HUD's income-limits page.
  • Food banks (TEFAP)Free groceries. Limits are set locally, often around 185% of the poverty line, and there's no asset test.
  • Legal aidA free civil lawyer for evictions, benefit denials or family cases, up to 125% of the poverty line ($19,950 for 1 person).

You work earned income

  • Earned Income Tax CreditA tax refund of up to $664 with no kids (ages 25–64) or up to $8,231 with 3+ kids, for tax year 2026.
  • Child Tax Credit$2,200 per child. Up to $1,700 of it is paid as a refund even if you owe no tax. You and your child need SSNs.
  • Child care subsidy (CCDF)Help paying daycare while you work or attend school. States can go up to 85% of state median income.
  • Free tax filing (VITA)Free tax prep for incomes up to roughly $67,000–$69,000, depending on the site.

Pregnant or a child under 5 185% FPL

  • WICFood, formula and nutrition help, up to $29,526 for 1 person (+$10,508 per extra person). Pregnancy counts the unborn baby in household size.
  • Pregnancy MedicaidEvery state covers pregnancy up to at least 138% of the poverty line, and many go to about 200%. This applies even in states that didn't expand Medicaid.
  • Head Start / Early Head StartFree preschool at or below the poverty line. You're automatically eligible if you're homeless, a foster family, or on TANF or SSI.

School-age kids 130–185%

  • Free / reduced-price school mealsFree up to 130% of the poverty line, reduced price (40¢ lunch) up to 185%. Kids in SNAP or TANF households get free meals automatically.
  • Summer EBT (SUN Bucks)$120 per child each summer for groceries, in participating states (most states take part).
  • CHIPHealth coverage for kids. Limits run from about 200% to over 300% of the poverty line, depending on the state.
  • TANFMonthly cash for families with kids. Limits are set by each state and are usually very low. There's a 60-month lifetime limit.

65+, blind or disabled seniors

  • SSIUp to $994/mo for 1 person or $1,491 for a couple in 2026. Assets must be under $2,000 ($3,000 for a couple). It unlocks Medicaid in most states.
  • Medicare Savings ProgramsPay your Part B premium and more. Income up to about $1,350–$1,816/mo for 1 person, with assets under $9,950.
  • Extra Help (Part D)Cuts drug costs, up to $23,940 a year in income ($18,090 in assets) for 1 person.
  • Senior food box (CSFP)A monthly grocery box at age 60+, up to 130–150% of the poverty line depending on the state.
  • Section 202 / 811 housingSubsidized apartments for low-income people 62+ (202) or with disabilities (811).

Bills, phone, school extras

  • LIHEAPHelp with heating and cooling bills, up to 150% of the poverty line ($23,940 for 1 person) or 60% of state median income.
  • WeatherizationFree insulation and repairs that lower your energy bills, up to 200% of the poverty line.
  • Lifeline$9.25/mo off a phone or internet bill, up to 135% of the poverty line ($21,546), or if you're on SNAP, Medicaid or SSI.
  • Pell GrantUp to $7,395 for college in 2026–27, which you don't pay back. Apply through FAFSA. College students face extra SNAP rules.
  • ACA marketplace subsidyLowers health premiums for incomes from 100% to 400% of the poverty line if you can't get Medicaid.

What changed in 2025–2026

A 2025 federal law (the "One Big Beautiful Bill Act") and new HUD proposals changed several of these programs.

In effect

SNAP work rules widened. Adults 18–64 without a child under 14 must work, volunteer or train 80 hours a month, or they lose SNAP after 3 months in a 3-year period. Veterans, homeless people and former foster youth no longer get an automatic exemption.

In effect

SNAP for immigrants narrowed. Refugees, asylees and several other groups lost eligibility. Citizens, most green-card holders (usually after 5 years), Cuban/Haitian entrants and COFA citizens can still get it.

By Jan 1, 2027

Medicaid work requirement. Expansion adults aged 19–64 will need 80 hours a month of work, school, volunteering or job training, or earnings of at least $580 a month. Pregnant people, the medically frail, some caregivers and others are exempt.

Since Jan 2026

ACA subsidies shrank. The extra pandemic-era help expired, so there's again no subsidy above 400% of the poverty line, and most people pay more.

Proposed

HUD housing rules. HUD has proposed letting housing authorities add work requirements and time limits of 2 years or more, and removing assistance from "mixed-status" immigrant families. Neither is final yet.

Proposed

LIHEAP elimination. The White House FY2027 budget would cut LIHEAP entirely. Congress has rejected this in past years, and the program is funded for 2026.

Ended

The Affordable Connectivity Program ($30/mo internet) ended in June 2024. Lifeline is what's left.
